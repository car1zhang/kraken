# Direct — One Agent, The Short Path

`/direct` is not a fourth phase. It is an override on the three phase skills — `.claude/skills/aim/SKILL.md`, `.claude/skills/info/SKILL.md`, `.claude/skills/do/SKILL.md` — for jobs small enough that the crew costs more than it buys. Everything those skills exist to produce still gets produced; what changes is WHO produces it (you, alone) and HOW MUCH of each skill you read (the sections this file points at, never the whole file). The goal is fewer tokens and a faster job, and the two rules that buy that are: spawn nothing, and read nothing you will not use.

## When It Applies, And For How Long

- A job spec that OPENS with `/direct` is a direct job from its first turn: Phase 1 (if the brief's doc-gating rule says one runs at all) and Phase 2 are both direct. The word after `/direct` may name the phase — `/direct aim`, `/direct info`, `/direct do` — or not: a bare `/direct` leaves the phase choice to the brief's doc-gating rule, exactly as a spec without it would. Any other word there (`/direct review`, `/direct pr`) names no phase and is read as bare `/direct` plus that word's own meaning (so `/direct review` is bare direct with the review opt-in on; a word with no meaning here is ignored).
- {{operator}} may also type it into your pane at any gate: `/direct do` at the `/do` gate makes Phase 2 direct even when Phase 1 ran with the crew; `/direct aim` or `/direct info` makes the next doc round direct. Typed while a crew round is still in flight, it takes effect at the next gate: the round in flight closes under the rules it was dispatched with (its agents are stopped on acceptance, not abandoned), and the next phase is the first direct one.
- Direct is STICKY for the rest of the job: a plain `/do` after a direct Phase 1 is a direct `/do`. {{operator}} leaves it only by asking for the full process in words ("full /do", "with the crew", "run the pipeline"), never by omission. Because a pane-typed `/direct` leaves no record outside this conversation, say `direct` (and whether review was requested) in every final message, and in the "Where we are" line of any `80-handoff.md` you write, so a compacted or resumed zeus does not fall back to the crew.
- **Review is off by default and on by request.** `/direct do review`, `review` as the word after the phase, or an explicit ask in the spec or a steer ("with review", "run the review round") turns the review round back on — see Phase 2 below. A reply at the manual-test gate that says {{operator}} will review it themselves is not that ask. Nothing else in a direct job spawns an agent.
- `solo: true` on a direct job makes the review opt-in UNAVAILABLE: solo means no teammates and no subagents at all, and that flag outranks a `review` word. (A solo job without `/direct` never reads this skill; its own no-crew rules are the brief's and the phase skills'.)

## The Overriding Rules

1. **No crew, no subagents.** No researcher subagents, no `hermes-<id>`, no `hephaestus`, no `athena-*` (the review opt-in below is the ONE exception), no `Explore`, no `Workflow`. You read the code, you write the artifacts, you implement, you write the tests, you verify. This overrides the brief's whole crew contract for the rest of the job — the "delegation is earned" gate, the hephaestus/athena split, and its rule that the athena review runs every time: here it runs only when asked.
2. **Read a phase skill by SECTION, never whole.** The three phase skills are long because they brief and arbitrate a crew; a direct job uses a few sections of each. Locate a section with `grep -n '^## Inside A Kraken Cell' .claude/skills/<phase>/SKILL.md` (or whichever heading a step below names) and `Read` from that line — with the Read tool, which is also what moves the board's stage badge — using a `limit` that stops at the next heading. Reading a phase skill whole is the token spend this skill exists to avoid.
3. **Prose-only artifacts.** No diagram cards, no fragments, no `lint_svg.py` — a direct set is `.md` files only, and nothing in it draws. When {{operator}} asks for a diagram by name, you do not render one and you do not spawn a renderer: say in your final message that a diagram takes the crew (a `hermes-<id>` teammate under aim's delegation rule) and that this job is direct, and let them choose — "full /aim" for that round, or no figure. `solo: true` forbids the renderer outright, whatever they choose.
4. **Everything else in the brief still binds** — the final-message TLDR, `kraken load set|clear` on every idle turn, the `kraken questions get` / `kraken feedback get` re-read on every resume (a feedback note is acted on and named in your final message, never marked — that column is the nightly pass' alone), `kraken mm get` before anything {{operator}} reads, flags, the never-declare-done rule, the self-destruction guard. Direct replaces the brief's crew and review paragraphs, and nothing else of it.
5. **Every md artifact still opens with a one-liner stating its main message**, and **never hard-wraps prose** — one paragraph, one bullet, one table row is ONE source line, however long. The set root stays operator-facing only: scratch goes to your scratchpad, never into `<cellDir>/artifacts/`.
6. **The phase's closing line is the phase skill's.** Each `Inside A Kraken Cell` section owns the exact line that ends its phase and what follows it; direct never restates one. Read that section (rule 2) and end on its line.

## Phase 1, Direct: `/direct aim` And `/direct info`

Research yourself: read the relevant `CLAUDE.md`/`AGENTS.md`, `rg` the entrypoints, read enough implementation to cite real paths. Stop when the artifact can be written responsibly — not when you could write the long version. Before the first artifact, the sibling-work check is unconditional and cheap: `kraken job list`, then `kraken artifacts get <slug> 00-overview.md` for any slug naming your system.

**`/direct aim`** (spec path): read aim's `## Artifact Set Shape` and `## Inside A Kraken Cell` sections. Both state a REQUIRED floor — three named section docs and a worked-example diagram card — and that floor does not apply here; this paragraph is the direct floor. Ship `00-overview.md` (the overview as aim describes it) plus ONE section doc, `50-plan.md`, carrying as three tables what aim's `50-components.md`, `51-code-map.md` and `52-testing-plan.md` carry — components (kind, responsibility, boundary, test plan), code map (where things live, review guide), and the testing plan (omit it for an explain-mode set, which proposes nothing to test). One file rather than three because the three exist to brief blind test authors, and a direct job has none; the content is still the implementation's contract. Open questions are still rows, still only genuine {{operator}} decisions, written through the `kraken questions` verbs in aim's Core Rules (`grep -n 'kraken questions set' .claude/skills/aim/SKILL.md` — the FIRST hit is the rule; Read that one bullet) — investigate before asking, bake in defaults, never block on an answer. End on aim's closing line and STOP; `/do` is {{operator}}'s move.

**`/direct info`** (explanation path): read info's `## First pass: one brief overview` table and its `## Inside A Kraken Cell` section. Ship `00-overview.md` alone — the answer, the mental model as a small table, one concrete flow, where it lives, a "go deeper" menu — and no diagram card. Later rounds add one `5N-<topic>.md` each. No open questions. Ever. End on info's closing line and STOP.

## Phase 2, Direct: `/direct do`

Read do's `## Inside A Kraken Cell` section and its `### 7. Write The Build Report` section; nothing else of do unless a step below sends you there.

1. **Plan** from the artifact set when Phase 1 ran, else from the job spec, which is the contract exactly as written. Read `00-overview.md` and EVERY other `.md` in the set root in full — `50-plan.md` for a direct set; `50-components.md`, `51-code-map.md`, `52-testing-plan.md` and whatever else is there for a set the crew wrote, skimming any diagram cards' captions — and re-run `kraken questions get <slug>` for answers. An answered question is a decision; an unanswered P0 gets your stated default, recorded in the build report, and `AskUserQuestion` only when a wrong guess makes the work useless.
2. **Implement yourself**, under the rules do states for an un-delegated slice, unchanged: edit repo files with the Edit tool, never through Bash; a destructive command runs alone, in its own call, after you re-read it; a comment asserts only what you checked this session; when a change retires a meaning, grep the repo for its remaining references once; never `git stash`, never a probe file in the worktree.
3. **Write the tests yourself**, from the plan's test tables or the spec's acceptance claims — there is no author to write them blind, and an un-delegated slice must not ship untested. Say in the build report that authorship was not split. When an earlier crew round on this slug left blind tests in the repo, they are still asserting: re-cover what they pin or delete the ones this change deliberately obsoletes, never leave them failing.
4. **Verify with the CI command, over the CI scope, on the CI merge base** — never a narrowed file list, never a suite filtered through `head`. For a UI change, drive the surface before reporting.
5. **Review: skipped unless requested.** When it was NOT requested, write `review skipped (direct)` in the build report's Verification row and move on. When it WAS, run do's review round on the finished tree: `athena-logic`, `athena-edge`, `athena-style` as three named teammates on `model: opus`, dispatched in one message as three fresh Agent calls (do's "ONE new Agent call" binding resumes two blind authors, which a direct job never has), each briefed with one dimension, the verdict bar verbatim (*BLOCKING means you would not ship the tree as it stands; CLEAR means every remaining item is optional*), its own scratch report path outside the repo, and the zsh dialect line. Then read do's `### 4. Plan The Implementation` step for three bullets: `**How to wait:` and its sub-bullet `**Wait for the whole fan-out in one watch` (one conjunctive watch over the report paths, `-s` not `-f`, end the turn, and what an expiry means) and `**A review round closes on verdicts` (the tree is frozen while a round runs; a verdict is the report file read IN FULL, never a grep, a tail or an idle ping; a post-verdict edit reopens the dimensions it touches). Fix blocking findings yourself; re-review with only the flagging variant(s); `TaskStop` each reviewer once its verdict is CLEAR, never on a BLOCKING one it still has to re-run. The round count and every blocking finding go in the build report.
6. **Write `90-build-report.md`** into the set root per do's section 7, every row present. The join row reads `nothing delegated (direct)`; Discrepancies, Complications, Deferred and Verification say "None" rather than vanish.
7. End on do's manual-test-gate line and STOP. When {{operator}}'s reply passes it, follow `.claude/skills/pr/SKILL.md` by path (never `/pr` by name), then STOP.

## Inside A Kraken Cell

This skill only ever runs in a cell, so the deltas above are already the cell's. Three reminders, because they are the three things a one-agent job drops:

- The set root is the brief's SET ROOT (`<cellDir>/artifacts/`), the only board-visible directory. Nothing is staged in `<cellDir>/doc/fragments/` — a direct job renders no fragments, ever.
- `kraken load set|clear` and the final-message TLDR fire on every idle turn exactly as in a crew job; a direct job that goes quiet with no estimate is a blocked card nobody can rank.
- Never declare the job done — accepting or merging is {{operator}}'s move, never yours.
