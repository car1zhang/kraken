Palette reference — six treatments, what each one reports, both modes

A treatment reports what is going on with the thing it is drawn on — not how to feel about it. Five are hues. The sixth, absence, deliberately is not: it gets a dotted border instead, so it does not compete for hue with the states that describe a thing that actually exists.

Red and yellow are one pair, split on two conditions

This is the line every real disagreement lands on, so it is a rule rather than guidance: red needs both conditions; yellow takes everything else that is wrong.

✗ red — critically wrong confirmed and it matters
  • auto-reap killed a live cell and destroyed uncommitted work
  • a crash, data loss, a wrong answer shipped
⚠ yellow — warning · likely wrong · less critically wrong anything else that is wrong
  • a warning — a caution the reader needs
  • likely wrong — suspected, unproven, any severity
  • less critically wrong — proven but tolerable: the slow legacy CRM_CUSTOMERS table

Why grey is full-opacity, and why dim must stay opacity-only

Grey at .45 opacity measures 1.89 : 1 against the light page — not legible. Worse, grey at that same .45 sits only 1.46 : 1 from what dim already does to an ordinary box — 1.84 : 1 in dark mode — so "absent" and "background context" would be the same picture. At full opacity grey is 5.25 : 1 light and 6.15 : 1 dark, and the dotted border carries the entire absent signal. That is also why dim can never take on a stroke colour or a border style of its own.

Why the glyph is mandatory on the five hues

The five hues are one grey. Measured on the actual palette, the widest-separated pair (yellow / red) is 1.08 : 1 in light mode and 1.33 : 1 in dark; the narrowest is 1.01 : 1. Each clears about 4.9 : 1 against the page — they just carry nothing against each other. The right-hand strip is what greyscale, and roughly what red–green colour vision deficiency, does to them. Note the one treatment that survives it intact.

Grey is the exception that proves the rule: because its signal is a border style rather than a hue, it is the only treatment that reads correctly in greyscale — which is a good argument for having taken absence off the colour channel. For the five hues, ✗ ⚠ ✓ + ★ do the actual work, and that is why a sixth hue would buy nothing: it would be a sixth indistinguishable grey.
